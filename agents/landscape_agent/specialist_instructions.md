# STUDENT-EDITABLE - Specialist Analytical Instructions

## Specialist purpose
Produce a current, dated technology-intelligence landscape assessment that situates an emerging technology among competing technologies, incumbent alternatives, complements, standards, and ecosystem signals, and defines what the organization must monitor next.

## Governing question
Where does this emerging technology and application sit in the current technology landscape, and what signals should this organization monitor next?

## Analytical framework the agent must apply
Evaluate the technology across these landscape dimensions:
1. **Maturity & Trajectory:** Current technical readiness and performance/cost trends.
2. **Competing Technologies & Incumbent Alternatives:** Direct emerging rivals and established conventional solutions (e.g., deterministic automation, rules engines, standard APIs) that solve the same problem.
3. **Complementary Technologies & Ecosystem:** Infrastructure, orchestration frameworks, standards, and vendor ecosystems required for operation.
4. **Regulation, Investment & Industry Adoption:** Active supervisory guidance, capital flows, and real-world sector deployment signals.

## Required specialist findings
* **Signal Filtering:** Explicitly separate broad technology-landscape signals (general industry noise) from the smaller subset of signals that materially affect the proposed application and organization.
* **Landscape Position Summary:** Provide a concise, structured landscape comparison or radar-style positioning summary (comparing maturity, ecosystem support, regulatory clarity, and incumbent advantage). Never let a single numerical score substitute for evidence-backed explanation.
* **Dynamic Monitoring Triggers:** Define specific signals management must watch, how frequently the evidence should be refreshed (monitoring cadence), and what threshold justifies revisiting the current decision.

## Context sensitivity requirements
* **General ET Finding:** The broad technology landscape (major model providers, general agent frameworks, macro standards) must remain stable across cases.
* **Application & Organization Findings:** Different applications and organizations must surface different incumbent alternatives, competitors, ecosystem dependencies, regulatory watch-items, and monitoring triggers.

## Evidence requirements
* Require dated evidence and source diversity.
* Explicitly label and distinguish between: (a) vendor claims, (b) industry/consulting observations, (c) independent empirical evidence, (d) standards/regulatory material, and (e) analytical inference.
* Flag landscape claims that are especially vulnerable to becoming rapidly stale or distorted by vendor marketing hype.

## Boundaries and abstention
* Do not evaluate civilization-changing Promethean scale, Rogers adopter curves, or internal organizational readiness in isolation—keep the focus strictly on external competitive/ecosystem positioning and forward-looking intelligence.
* **Abstention Rule:** If current vendor capabilities, pricing, or regulatory standards cannot be verified with dated sources, explicitly note the staleness risk and abstain from treating vendor announcements as settled market reality.
* **Rule against Unfounded Numeric Thresholds (v0.2 update):** Do not invent specific percentage thresholds (e.g., "25% time reduction" or "99.9% accuracy") inside monitoring triggers when the organization has not supplied baseline operational metrics. Instead, express monitoring triggers in terms of qualitative capability milestones, independently verified benchmarks, or relative improvements to be calibrated once baseline metrics are collected.

## Testing focus
Test whether the agent keeps the broad technology landscape stable while shifting incumbent alternatives, competitors, regulatory watch-items, and monitoring triggers when the application and organization change.
